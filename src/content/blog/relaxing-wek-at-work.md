**Monday**

This class looked like an Internet cafe in that there were many computers set up in a few rows.  It makes me think of an Internet cafe I remember going to when I was much younger.  I think it was called Cyber Connections.  At the time, the game was Counter Strike.  A huge LAN party of some sorts.  Each computer was much more up to date and had better graphics than anything we had at home.  We could play for $5 an hour.  It was me, my brother and two cousins.  We didn't go too often, but I remember such a good feeling of being able to all play together at the same time.

The students here were working on tests or missing work.  I think attendance was about 50% for each class.  I did not understand what the class really was.  It seemed like another failure in the school system.  The kids stayed quiet and some worked.  I was able to maintain some privacy in how the classroom was arranged.  It gave me time to catch up on my writing here and to take a look at the MIT free online courses.

I did not find exactly what I was hoping for, but I can see how useful this website and the materials can be.  Most of the curses are for engineering and mathematics, which makes sense because of the school's focus.  I did find courses on Spanish, Music, Drama, and Photography.  I suppose all universities offer at least some variety of courses.  I listened to a recording from about 1969 of a person reading a short paragraph.  He then recorded the recording and repeated this process until you couldn't make out any words, just sounds of the room's resonance through speech.  It was an interesting experiment.  I understand the process, but never took it that far and put together that when we record over a recording, the vibrations from the room begin to come into play.

I also spent some time listening to a podcast and doing a few of my one a day origami crafts.  I liked the organ base and the house the best out of the few I did.  I didn't understand the building and the elephant instructions.  I messed them up.  The bomber plane was cool, complicated, and couldn't fly.  Otherwise, It would have been a good one, too.  

**Tuesday**

So far, this class makes little sense to me as well. There are six school periods, and three adults are in the room at all times.  The largest class size is nine and the smallest is four.  I understand this is a SPED class, meaning there are multiple grade levels represented here.  So far, In the first two periods, I see only one student who looks like she is doing any school work.  The other students are on their phones.  One of the adult aides is helping her.  The other one went away.  It was a bit odd, the teacher was here a few minutes before class started.  I heard him say he is going to a training meeting.  He asked one of the students to gather 10 Chromebooks from another classroom.  For some reason this classroom does not have any issued to it.  It is strange to me to ask the students to do this when I just witnessed what looked like two adults doing almost nothing at their desks for a whole class period.  It is understandable that they may be working on paperwork, but I think I saw them on their phones too.

I noticed that when I went to take attendance that it was already entered.  I asked one of the adults and they said they will take care of it themselves.  That works for me, but now I am seeing another seemingly wasted use of money.  There are two fully grown adults here, and the main teacher is out training for his profession.  I do not understand why I am required to be here.  It is completely understandable to have a teacher step in, but it seems like a waste when there doesn't seem to be a need for it.  I suppose not every class can run well when the main teacher is out, but this one, with so few kids, it is hard to imagine that they even need me here.

It is only the second period, so perhaps I am making too much of an assumption.

During 3rd period, one of the aides put on CNN 10 for today.  She used YouTube instead of the actual website though: we had ads.  Today's topics were not too exciting for me.  It did remind me to look for a mostly unbiased news outlet I can access for free.  I found Reuters and BBC seem to be the most well-known.  I did another search to find Straight Arrow News, SAN.  I like SAN's format for sure.  I can see a grid of the latest news without the distractions on the left and right of the screen.  I also like that it keeps everything the same size, too.  No favoritism shown through size.

I had a decent conversation with a student who moved out here this year from a more popular city and wants to be a boxer.  He has been boxing since 3rd grade.  He has been in trouble for fighting.  He lives with his dad.  His mom stayed closer to that city and plans to move again.  He needs about $120 a month to continue boxing.  He makes some money with a friend who buys CVVs and it's a gamble to have money on it.

**Thursday**

This class has preferred seating, meaning that there are not just desks and chairs.  Some students might work on a couch, a bean bag, or on the floor.  Not much happened with this class.  The work was fairly involved, not just a simple worksheet.  I do not think many of them really worked.  I only got a handful of them turned in from two class periods.  I was there for four periods, and just a few minutes of the 5th period. The teacher came in just as I was taking attendance.  I was excited to be able to leave early again.  Perhaps I went back to the office too quickly.  I got to the office and was assigned another room.  The other substitute who was supposed to be in that room, was nowhere to be found.  The positive thing is that I am now covering one class for a former teacher of mine.

Side note to myself:  As of yesterday, this school has made it known to all staff that students are not allowed to get passes to go to other classes.  This saves me some time and effort.  It makes more sense for students to stay in class if the class has any structure.  Sometimes it is easier or better to let them go to another class though.

For only one class period, I got to cover for a former teacher of mine.  I took his class during my freshman year, for AP World History.  It is the only AP test that I took.  I failed so badly.  I do not blame anyone.  I could say the teacher didn't prepare us well, although I believe a few students got the highest scores, or I could say I wasn't ready or serious enough to pass an AP test as a freshman.

This class is easy enough.  I came in much later than I prefer.  The students were already in class, as a security guard had let them in.  The students were mostly quiet.  Only about six of them are even talking.  They are not doing the work, which seems to be: watch and listen to a YouTube history video and they are to write down 20 facts from the video.  It is a history of World War 2.  

At least two of these students are gambling.  I heard and saw dice.  I saw a few dollar bills.  I understand that this school and perhaps another one seem to have this gambling problem going on during school hours.  I suppose these types of activities have always happened on school campuses across the world and for as long as public schools have been established.  These kids are looking to earn some money, I think.  Some probably like the thrill of gambling itself.  Of course, the dangers of gambling will not escape these students.

This same group of students is now trying to make some prank phone calls.
