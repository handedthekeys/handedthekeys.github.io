**Week 5 2026**

**Music**

Last week I finished up my 10th consecutive day in Mr. E's class. It is rare that I would do something like this without knowing the class, but I saw that it was a music class at a school that I rarely have any troubles, so I went for it. I do not regret it at all. I enjoyed that class more than usual. I did not have to do much, but I did because I actually knew a little bit of what I was doing.

There were 6 class periods with students: two guitar classes, and four band classes. I really liked that the band classes had student conductors. This helped so much because the teacher already set up a system that gave some authority to one of their peers so that everything wasn't solely on me to maintain. Normally this is fine, but I imagine a stranger coming into this class with no music experience whatsoever would have a really hard time. I appreciate the teacher for that.

The first day, I immediately made it known to the students that I was expected to be there for the full 10 days. Unfortunately, sometimes students have to endure a new person everyday when the teacher has to be out for an extended amount of time. I started on a Friday. I wanted to make sure the students knew that I understood enough music to know if they were doing well or not.

I understood that I could have probably just sat at the desk and let the student conductors do what they wanted for the class, but I knew that if left to themselves, chaos and injuries might occur. Usually being in a class for just one day won't cause too much trouble. Kids like to be able to relax a bit more when they have a substitute teacher. I don't blame them, but this time, I was going to be there for a little while.

**Here's the stage:** the room was larger than most classrooms, as to be expected, but definitely not a band or choir room, with built-in stage tiered seating or scaffolding. There were a few music posters around the room and instruments everywhere. I imagine many band rooms struggle to have appropriate storage. I did like that the guitars were along the walls in racks built for five each. There was one upright piano and a desk in the corner. At the front of the room there was a large touchscreen all-in-one computer and whiteboard.

**Lesson plans:** The lesson plans for this class were a bit vague, but I had plenty of information to understand what we needed to do. I imagine he wrote these in a bit of a hurry as the 10 days had much of the same instructions repeated, instead of being more detailed for each day. Four class types, and each day had almost the exact same wording for the instructions, just with different assignments.

I would have liked to have been given more of the rules that he likes to keep in class. There are so many nuanced procedures or customs in a classroom that no one can pick up on without being in the class. I also do not want to be told how to do things by the students, even though they may be 100% correct, it is very hard to trust a student when a teacher has left no indication of the expectations.

I often look for classroom rules posted on the walls if not in the lesson plans. I then observe the students to see if they follow those rules. They almost always follow at least one of the rules that might be unique to the class. No eating was one of the rules, but not all students followed that one. When I saw a student eating red chips and playing guitar at the same time, I was shocked. If this was a hard rule the teacher followed, other students would have chimed in when they saw someone eating. If it was my music class, I think this is a very good rule to keep. I would want the kids to respect the instruments better than to eat in the room, especially near their school instruments.

**What did I do:** I already did my best to make sure the students knew I would be around. I tried to learn names, and I did learn at least a few each class, by the end of the assignment. I like to do my best to say student's names correctly. When I take attendance vocally, instead of by a seating chart, I have to say some very strange names sometimes. A few times, I cannot even begin to understand how to pronounce them. Those names seem to be from the Pacific Islands or in some cases, Asian. I guess I can do a little research to find out if I can learn how to pronounce them.

By the second day, I was able to see which student conductors knew what they were doing and which did not. I was expecting them to actually be students who wanted to be conductors or maybe had some training. From how most of them behaved, a student conductor was the person in charge of how often the group repeated a song and they set the tempo. Each student conductor had some skills and some areas to improve. I didn't have to do much for the guitar classes. Band is where I needed to do more.

I felt like I could not conduct because I never had any training. What I noticed a few days in, is that the student's music books gave tips on how to conduct. I found the conductor's book for all the music the band students were working on. I could have tried to learn as much as I could from the book, but I didn't really think of that since I doubt I would be conducting again this year. I spent some of my time during the prep period and lunch practicing my ukulele instead. The last day, in one of the classes, some students asked me to conduct.

I knew enough to be able to look at the sheet music and follow along to the metronome. There is a lot to being a conductor. My experience had been in the choir classes I took in high school and in college. I really admired those conductors and knew they loved music.

Honestly, I know I have issues with timing. I did not want to mess anyone up. When the students asked me, I did feel honored and even capable, but I was afraid to step into that role and be exposed. Or, at least that is how I felt. I also don't really know what it means to be a conductor. I know that each person will have their own style, but not until after seeing that book, did I realize that there is a basic framework to what direction to move your hands or the baton for each time signature and tempo.

**Special notes:** I only saw one previous student from when I was a 5th grade teacher. I did, however, have a few short but meaningful talks with a few students. One student was very personable and seemed to have good people skills. She was likable. The first day I was there she came to visit before school started. She was expecting her teacher, but she came in anyway and we talked for a moment.

Almost every morning she would come by. I liked that. I think it was the third day she came by and I asked her name. Then it took me two days more to realize she was in one of the classes! In my defense, she told me her nickname, and not her given name. I guess I just thought, she looked so much like that girl from the morning.
